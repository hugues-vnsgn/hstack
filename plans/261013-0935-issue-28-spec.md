# Issue 28: usage controls and interrupted runs

## Overview

Reduce wasted AI usage and recover interrupted work while preserving the bundled
pstack workflow. This spec records the owner's interview decisions and was confirmed as a whole
before implementation. Its acceptance criteria remain the approved baseline.

The baseline is pstack 0.15.9, revision
`e43c7ee26e0038c6c1fa8380dd34ce86ff94cb2a`, recorded in
[the upstream pin](../docs/upstream/pin.json). The source was inspected from
`provenance/upstream/pstack-0.15.9.tar.gz` without extracting or executing it.
This is the bundled baseline, not a claim about today's upstream main.

## Requirements

### Owners and helpers

- Default to one active PR owner. More owners require the owner's explicit
  choice. A PR owner is distinct from its reviewers and helper workers.
- Run one required arena or review group at a time. Keep the configured pstack
  participant count, parallel steps and required checks. The approved program
  plan states the group's size and reviewer/helper limits before work starts.
- Count the coordinator, PR owners, reviewers and nested helpers in program
  usage accounting. Additional nested groups require authorization; helpers
  cannot expand their own limits.
- Hold a required step that cannot fit the approved limits for the owner's
  decision. Preserve the required participants and checks.
- Give workers the relevant brief and accessible evidence references. Preserve
  pstack's complete task contracts and standing orders; avoid copying large logs
  and the whole program into every helper's instructions.

These scheduling limits are owner-approved huguesStack additions. Upstream
pstack runs independent PR owners in parallel. The additions preserve its
required workflow participants and proof obligations.

### Usage and stop behavior

- Use host-provided session and weekly allowance information when available.
  Report missing allowance as unknown and continue within the approved limits.
- Record available usage across all participants. Distinguish complete, partial
  and unknown totals. Preserve input, output and cache distinctions; avoid
  double-counting resumed totals or nested activity.
- Token totals do not establish remaining subscription allowance or exact
  subscription cost. No universal numerical token ceiling is approved.
- Follow the selected pstack playbook's existing timebox and stopping rules.
  Orchestrate stops spawning around 70% of its planned duration. This is a time
  rule for that workflow, not a subscription percentage or a new cutoff for
  every playbook.
- Apply no artificial subscription percentage cutoff. The owner rejected the
  proposed 80% cutoff in Q10.
- Usage warnings are recorded and reported; they do not introduce an automatic
  pause. In Q13 the owner chose the bundled pstack rule rather than the proposed
  warning-triggered stop. This supersedes Q6's earlier agreement.
- Explicit owner stops and existing playbook stop conditions remain effective.
  A host quota rejection may force interruption. Regular checkpoints support
  recovery when no cleanup turn can run.

The inspected pstack source has no automatic subscription-warning pause rule.
Its safe-pause recipe says "explicit only"; its router also lists going offline,
restart and imminent context compaction. This spec does not add a quota trigger.

### Saved progress and restart

- Save progress after completed steps and before handing work to another agent.
  Before a long action, record what is about to run and its recovery point.
- Keep a permanent local progress record with approved work and decisions,
  owner stop instructions, completed changes and proof, unfinished steps and
  the next action, workers and device owners, and available usage information.
  Link large artifacts and mark unavailable information unknown.
- When a new session starts, reconcile saved progress against actual completed
  work and workers that may still be running. Continue previously authorized
  work only when its state can be established safely.
- Preserve completed work. Mark interrupted steps unfinished and inspect their
  actual effects before repeating them. Avoid duplicate workers and actions.
- Hold affected work when worker state is uncertain. An explicit owner stop
  survives restart and prevents automatic continuation.
- Preserve host-provided resume behavior where available. A durable pickup
  path must also work from a fresh session; no independent automatic wake
  service is required by this spec.

### Waiting and device recovery

- Wait through a completion notification when available; otherwise use one
  blocking process. Repeated model turns asking whether a worker or device is
  free are outside the approved waiting behavior. Preserve required pstack
  audits and checks.
- If neither waiting mechanism is available, save resume instructions and
  report the blockage.
- Keep a device reservation while its owning process runs. After a worker dies,
  confirm that it and its device-using processes have stopped.
- Record the test settings before changing them. Restore those settings and
  verify restoration before releasing the reservation. Preserve app data and
  evidence.
- Hold the affected device when ownership or restoration cannot be established
  safely. The existing progress-store lock is distinct from device ownership.

## Constraints

- Public skill names, required pstack phases, configured model roles and proof
  requirements remain intact. This task does not upgrade the upstream baseline.
- Approved requirements and plugin write protection from fixes 1 and 2 remain
  in force. Budget pressure cannot weaken pass conditions or erase failures.
- Source checks establish source and wiring only. Real Claude Code and Codex
  trials occur after the remaining approved fixes, as requested by the owner.
- Preventing subscription exhaustion and automatically pausing before quota
  rejection are outside the approved behavior. Disk-capacity and device-queue
  optimization remain outside this issue.

## Interface contract

The program plan states its selected playbook, owner limit, required group sizes,
reviewer/helper limits, applicable timeboxes and permanent progress location.
Changes to approved limits require an explicit owner decision.

Status and pickup reports name completed and pending work, the next action,
known workers and device ownership, evidence locations, usage coverage, unknown
allowance and unresolved decisions. Missing information must stay visible.

Implementation may extend the existing shared host checks and progress store.
This spec does not require a new public skill or an account-wide coordination
service across unrelated programs.

## Acceptance criteria

| Scenario | Required result |
|---|---|
| A second PR owner starts without authorization | Dispatch is held; the agreed limit remains intact. |
| A required review or arena group is prepared | Every configured participant remains present and its complete size fits the approved plan. |
| A helper attempts an undeclared nested group | Dispatch is held without reducing required coverage. |
| Host allowance or token totals are missing | The report says unknown or partial; approved work continues within applicable limits. |
| Nested or resumed usage is recorded | Every participant is accounted for where measurable, without counting the same activity twice; gaps remain visible. |
| Usage crosses 80%, or the host emits a warning | No new percentage-based or warning-based pause is introduced. Existing owner and playbook stops still apply. |
| Orchestrate reaches its existing planned-time spawn cutoff | It follows its existing rule, without treating time as subscription usage. |
| The coordinator is interrupted mid-step | Fresh-session pickup preserves completed work, identifies unfinished effects and avoids duplicate workers. |
| An owner stop precedes restart | No automatic continuation occurs. |
| A process still owns a device | Its reservation is not reclaimed, including during restart. |
| A worker dies after changing test settings | Verify its processes have stopped, restore and check settings, preserve app data and proof, then release. Uncertainty holds the device. |
| A worker or device is unavailable | Notification or a blocking process handles waiting; no repeated model-generated status polling. Missing capability produces a durable handoff. |
| A required proof check is missing after interruption | It remains missing or blocked, rather than becoming a pass. |
